How To Eat An Apple Properly (I Am The Only One With Correct Opinions On This)

Hello, folks! It has come to my attention recently that some people have been expressing opinions on how to eat apples that are not entirely synonymous with mine.

Since my opinions on how to eat apples are, as far as I have discovered, the only correct ones, this fact is naturally very distressing to me. I cannot imagine how these poor souls could have stumbled so far into darkness, but out of the goodness of my heart I have written this guide to bring them back into the light. My qualifications are thus: ever since I can remember, and possibly since I have had teeth (I don't remember), I have been eating apples with a passion and voracity that frightened even my parents. I do not know how many apples I eat in a year, though I would not be surprised if the number was in the hundreds. I do not know how many apples I have eaten in my lifetime, but it is enough to have developed a sophisticated technique for maximising enjoyment and minimising mess.

If you are one of these misguided souls with incorrect opinions, please listen carefully. This is very important.

Eating apples falls into two separate but equally sophisticated disciplines: cut, and handheld.

Handheld

  1. Before you start, it is imperative to name your apple as a gesture of respect.
    1. Twist the stem. Recite the alphabet, one letter per twist.
    2. When the stem breaks, that letter is the first letter of your apple's name. The first name that comes into your head that starts with that letter is your apple's name. If you eat a lot of apples you will start to get a lot of repeat names, so you're allowed to cheat a little. Or switch to a cultivar of apple with a different length and/or torsion strength of stem to switch up the most common letter. Or recite the alphabet backwards. If you're with someone else, it's common courtesy to ask them to name your apple, though if they're not familiar you may have to explain this entire procedure to them first and sometimes it's just not worth the social hassle.
    3. Suppose your apple's name is Steve. Say out loud to yourself or (if necessary, when in public) quietly in your head: "This apple's name is Steve."
    4. Say out loud to yourself or (if necessary, when in public) quietly in your head: "Goodbye, Steve!"
    5. Immediately after saying this, you will feel the crushing guilt for having brought Steve into the world, given him animacy and personhood and a name, only to immediately and brutally rend his flesh from his body while he watches.
    6. You can, at least, make Steve's death a kind and painless one. Gently prick Steve's skin with your thumbnail as a sort of symbolic anaesthetisation. If your apple has a sticker, this is a good opportunity to get rid of the sticker.
    7. Wait two to five seconds for the anasthetic to take effect, and you're ready to eat!
  2. Select your entry site. This should be just slightly below the equator of the apple, enough to leave margins on both the top and bottom of the apple but with a significantly larger margin on the top.
  3. Bite! If your apple is particularly juicy, it might be a good idea to gently swipe over the site with your tongue to remove any risk of dripping. If your apple is really juicy, you can turn it upwards and look at the juice pooling in the concavity left by the bite, and isn't that neat.
  4. Bite a ring around the equator of the apple. At this point, the apple will look more or less like your picture-book apple core, though a little top-heavy. Please note that it is imperative for each bite to come behind the last, i.e. rotating the apple away from you, so that your chin is always contacting unbitten apple until the very last equator-bite.

FAQs

  • What if my apple doesn't have a stem?
  • Pick your favourite letter and proceed as usual. This is your chance to even out the letter frequencies a bit, so be strategic.
  • Do you really eat all your apples like this?
  • Every single one. If I'm forced to eat an apple outside of the procedure detailed here I get significantly distressed. That sounds like a joke but it's not a joke.
  • What about pears?
  • Pears are an equally fascinating and nuanced subject to which I could dedicate another page, but I will say that the general shape of the procedure is similar, the main difference - and difficulty - being in the treatment of the main bulb of flesh vs the elongated cone around the stem, the latter of which requires considerable respect. The most striking difference in the treatment of pears and apples is that it is im-pear-ative to name your pears using pear-themed names: Peardita, Peariwinkle, Pearicles Prince of Tyre, and so forth.
  • What about peaches?
  • Don't even get me started on peaches.
  • So have you ever thought about getting diagnosed for... something?
  • Oh, honey. I don't need a doctor to tell me how much fun I'm having.
  • You can't anaesthetise an apple using your thumbnail.
  • Apples also can't be named Steve and watch in horror as their flesh is sundered from their core. We all have to compromise on our planes of reality a bit here. Okay? Okay.
